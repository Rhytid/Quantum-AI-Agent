A SOLUTION TO HAAGERUP’S PROBLEM AND POSITIVE HAHN-BANACH SEPARATION THEOREMS IN
OPERATOR ALGEBRAS
IKHAN CHOI
ABSTRACT. We affirmatively resolve a question posed by Uffe Haagerup in 1975 on the positive version of the bipolar theorem
on the dual spaces of C∗-algebras. As a direct consequence, we obtain a complete set of four positive Hahn-Banach separation
theorems on von Neumann algebras, their preduals, C∗-algebras, and their duals. Furthermore, with the idea used to solve
the problem, we simplify Haagerup’s original solution to Dixmier’s problem on normal weights.

2. PRELIMINARIES
2.1.Suppression by the one-parameter family of functional calculi.The first step of the proof is the reformulation
of the theorem into an inclusion problem. This idea is due to Haagerup. More precisely , we can transform each
positive bipolar theorem equivalently to a statement of the form( F−E +)+⊂F. In other words, whenx i is a net
inF−E + convergent tox≥0 in a partially oredered real locally convex spaceE, there is a majorizing nety i∈F
satisfyingx i≤y i, we then need to findy∈Fsuch thatx≤yin order to deducex∈Fwith the hereditarity ofF.
An obstacle of this argument is that a nety i has of course no limit points in general. To address this, we suppress
the nety i to make it bounded with the following one-parameter family of real functions.
Definition 2.1.Forδ>0, we define a functionf δ :(−δ−1,∞)→Rsuch that
fδ(t):=t(1+δt) −1,t>−δ −1.
It has many interesting properties such as operator monotonicity and concavity , strong convergence to the identity as
δ→0, and the semi-group property in the sense thatf δ(fδ′ (t)) =f δ+δ′ (t)on a suitable domain oft∈R. We will only
use the operator monotonicity and the boundedness from above, given each fixedδ>0. With these functions, for
example in the situation of (1), we can suppress the nety i to define a bounded netf δ(y i)of positive elements. This
net is bounded above byy i∈Fso thatf δ(y i)∈Fby the hereditarity ofF, and we can take aσ-weakly convergent
subnet to getf δ(x)∈Ffromf δ(x i)≤f δ(y i)∈Fby the closedness and the hereditarity ofF. Then, the limitδ→0
and the closedness ofFwill givex∈F.
However, when we try to apply this strategy , we encounter two technical issues. One is that the netx i should
be bounded for the netf δ(x i)to be well-defined for a sufficiently small but fixedδ >0. It can be overcome by
Haagerup’s trick involving the Krein-Šmulian theorem. The other is, unlike theσ-strong topology that Haagerup
used, that we cannot expect the operation of taking functional calculus to be continuous with respect to theσ-weak
topology . For instance, when a self-adjoint netxi∈B(H) sa satisfies an assumption−(2δ)−1≤x i for alliandx i→x
