Abstract
Quantum teleportation is one of the most important subjects in quantum information science. This is because quantum teleportation
can be regarded as not only quantum information transfer but also a building block for universal quantum information processing.
Furthermore, deterministic quantum information processing is very important for efﬁcient processing and it can be realized with
continuous-variable quantum information processing. In this review, quantum teleportation for continuous variables and related
quantum information processing are reviewed from these points of view.

2. CV quantum teleportation
2.1. Theoretical background of CV quantum teleportation
Quantum teleportation for qubit systems was originally proposed by Bennett et al., in 1993 [8]. Vaidman extended
the concept to general quantum states, i.e., continuous variables[9]. Braunstein and Kimble proposed the experimental
scheme with squeezed states [10]. Moreover, the scheme was extended to atomic systems [11–13]. We will brieﬂy
introduce the Braunstein–Kimble scheme here [14].

3.3. Quantum telecloning
Quantum telecloning is an application of another type of tripartite quantum entanglement; the CV W-like state, which
has bipartite and tripartite quantum entanglement simultaneously.
Quantum telecloning [32] is a quantum information protocol combining cloning and teleporting into a single new
primitive. Here the cloning process produces approximate clones beyond the classical limit of ﬁdelity but they never
reach the perfect ones because of the no-cloning theorem [51]. The protocol offers signiﬁcant technical advantages
over the two-step local cloning plus teleportation strategy. In particular, in the case of coherent state telecloning, only
ﬁnite entanglement is required for generating remote clones with optimal ﬁdelity [33].
In fact, quantum telecloning generalizes quantum teleportation with multiple receivers[32]. In quantum teleportation,
bipartite quantum entanglement shared by two parties (Alice and Bob) enables them to teleport an unknown quantum
state from Alice to Bob by communicating only through classical channels as shown in previous subsections. If three
parties (Alice, Bob, and Claire) share an appropriate tripartite entangled state, Alice is able to teleport an unknown

Realization scheme of the cubic phase gate is shown inFig. 21. This scheme consists of four parts of Gaussian operations

which can be realized by squeezers and the linear optical elements and can be regarded as the process of CV quantum
teleportation.

5. Future prospects
Up to here, we have showed the basics and the recent progress on CV quantum teleportation and related quantum
information processing. We believe that we will be able to make universal CV quantum information processing in the
future with the presented techniques here. Moreover, since CV quantum information is higher dimensional version of
qubits, the success of universal CV quantum information processing means the success of universal qubit quantum
information processing. For example, we can convert CV quantum information to qubits as follows:
|0⟩= c(|/afii9825⟩+|− /afii9825⟩),
|1⟩= c(|/afii9825⟩−|− /afii9825⟩), (32)
where the important thing here is that |0⟩and |1⟩are orthonormal. Recently, creation of c(|/afii9825⟩−|− /afii9825⟩), so-called
Schrödinger kitten, with photon subtraction technique is reported [47–50] and the example is shown in Fig. 25.B y
using this type of qubits, we also will be able to realize universal qubit quantum information processing.
As mentioned everywhere in this review, the most fundamental quantum information processing is quantum telepor-
tation. So we should pursuit to improve the performance.
